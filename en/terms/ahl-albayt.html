The Explicit Qur'anic Definition of "Ahl al-Bayt"

Allah says of the angels, addressing Abraham's wife, Sarah:

"Are you amazed at the decree of Allah? May the mercy of Allah and His blessings be upon you, people of the house. Indeed, He is Praiseworthy and Honorable."

And He says, addressing the wives of the Prophet ﷺ:

"And abide in your houses and do not display yourselves as [was] the display of the former times of ignorance. And establish prayer and give zakah and obey Allah and His Messenger. Allah intends only to remove from you the impurity [of sin], O people of the [Prophet's] household, and to purify you with [extensive] purification."

The highest degree of Qur'anic interpretation is interpreting the Qur'an by the Qur'an itself, and in these two verses there is explicit textual evidence that the intended meaning of the term "ahl al-bayt" here is the wives, and no one else.

And in Allah's words concerning the deliverance of Lot, peace be upon him:

"So We saved him and his family, except for his wife; she was of those who remained [with the evildoers]."

Here the word "ahlahu" (his family) came inclusive of his wife, and she was excluded only on account of her disbelief — not because she fell outside the scope of the word "ahl" in this verse.

We must also remember that the Qur'an was revealed to a Bedouin Arab nation that grasped meanings by its own natural linguistic instinct, without needing complexity or philosophizing. This nation's sensitivity to the meanings of words and the fine details of vocalization was extraordinarily high — so much so that a single error in the vocalization of one letter of the Qur'an would deeply alarm a fluent Arab, because he would instantly grasp that the meaning had flipped into something impermissible to say. Consider Allah's words in Surah At-Tawbah:

"...that Allah is disassociated from the disbelievers, and [so is] His Messenger."

With the genitive case ("wa-rasulihi"), the meaning is that His Messenger ﷺ is likewise disassociated from the disbelievers. But if someone were to read it in the nominative case ("wa-rasuluhu"), the meaning would flip into Allah being disassociated from His own Messenger — something impossible to say — and this is exactly why our eloquent predecessors were so alarmed whenever they heard it recited incorrectly. If this nation was this precise in understanding the vocalization of a single letter, how could it possibly be assumed that they would err or overlook the understanding of a complete, explicit meaning in a verse addressing them directly — such as the verse on "ahl al-bayt"?
